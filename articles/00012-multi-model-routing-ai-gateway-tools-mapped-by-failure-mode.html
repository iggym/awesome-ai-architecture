Multi-Model Routing & AI Gateway Tools, Mapped by Failure Mode

Research window: 2026-02-05 → 2026-03-12 Format: reference
AI gateways have split into two camps: zero-ops marketplaces for teams wanting unified model access, and self-hosted control planes for teams treating routing as infrastructure. The middle is empty.
⚠ Exclusion Criteria

This list deliberately leaves things out, and that's the point. Hyperscaler platforms like AWS Bedrock and Azure API Management offer model access, but routing is incidental to their marketplace function — they belong on a different list. IDE-embedded routers (Cursor, Entelligence, Factory) are thin model-selection wrappers, not independent routing infrastructure, and evaluating them against full gateways is a category error. Observability-first tools that added routing as an afterthought (Helicone) are excluded because their routing layer has no meaningful differentiation from the tools they proxy. We also skip "AI firewall" products that gate but don't route. What remains is a deliberately narrow set: tools whose primary value proposition is deciding which model handles which request, and doing it reliably at production scale. If you came here looking for the broadest possible survey, this isn't it.

Single-Provider Dependency

The moment your application logic talks directly to one provider's API, their rate limit becomes your outage and their deprecation becomes your migration project.

OpenRouter
Addresses provider lock-in for product teams shipping fast who need instant access to 400+ models with built-in auto-fallback through one key.
lock-in
LiteLLM
Addresses provider lock-in for infrastructure teams who want a self-hosted proxy supporting 100+ providers under a single OpenAI-compatible endpoint.
lock-in
Bifrost
Addresses throughput bottlenecks for performance-critical teams who want the fastest open-source Go-based gateway to absorb high-RPS agentic traffic.
lock-in
Vercel AI Gateway
Addresses onboarding friction for Next.js shops who want zero-config model access with no markup, at the cost of platform lock-in.
lock-in

Uncontrolled Spend

LLM pricing tiers are opaque, and most teams discover their cost profile only after the invoice lands.

RouteLLM
Addresses blind spend by training custom routing models that send simple queries to cheaper endpoints, reserving frontier models for hard prompts.
spend
Portkey
Addresses uncontrolled enterprise spend by bundling semantic caching, guardrails, and cost attribution into a managed control plane.
spend
OpenRouter
Addresses price discovery for teams who want to benchmark real token costs across providers without maintaining their own metering.
spend

Hybrid Deployment Complexity

The cleanest architectures assume every model is reachable via HTTPS. Reality involves fine-tuned weights on local GPUs and air-gapped inference servers.

ngrok AI Gateway
Addresses hybrid routing by offering a single hosted endpoint that reaches both public providers and privately-hosted models without exposing ports.
hybrid
LiteLLM
Addresses hybrid complexity for teams who want to self-host a proxy that routes identically to Bedrock, Azure, and a private vLLM instance.
hybrid

Edge Security Gaps

Once LLM traffic leaves your VPC, caching, rate limiting, and DLP become either your problem to build or someone else's problem to guarantee.

Cloudflare AI Gateway
Addresses edge leakage for teams already on Cloudflare who want zero-infrastructure caching, rate limiting, and DLP without deploying new software.
edge
Portkey
Addresses governance gaps for teams that need semantic caching, prompt versioning, and guardrails as a managed service rather than in-house plumbing.

Honest Gap Note (as of March 2026)

No mainstream gateway yet offers a genuinely unified control plane for both LLM and MCP tool traffic that doesn't sacrifice routing intelligence in the process. The agentic era is producing agent-to-agent communication patterns that existing routers weren't designed to govern, and the tools that claim to handle both are usually weak at one. Evaluators shopping for an "agentic gateway" should expect to assemble two layers, not buy one.
